The complete guide to skin fades

What to ask your barber, the different types of fades, and how to keep it looking fresh between visits.

A skin fade is one of the most popular men's haircuts for good reason — it's clean, sharp, and suits almost every face shape. But not all fades are the same, and getting the right one starts with knowing what to ask.

What is a skin fade?

A skin fade (also called a bald fade) blends your hair down to the skin at the shortest point. The transition can start at different heights on your head, which is where the different fade types come in.

The four main types of fade

Low fade: The blend starts just above the ears. It's subtle and works well for professional or conservative settings. Great if you're trying a fade for the first time.

Mid fade: The blend starts around the temple area. This is the most versatile option — noticeable without being too aggressive.

High fade: The blend starts high on the sides, near the crown. Bold and modern, this fade makes a statement and pairs well with textured or longer styles on top.

Drop fade: The fade curves down behind the ear rather than going straight around. It follows your natural head shape and adds a unique contour.

Taper vs fade — what's the difference?

A taper gradually shortens the hair but doesn't go down to the skin. A fade takes it all the way to zero. If you want the cleanest, sharpest look, ask for a fade. If you prefer a softer finish, go for a taper.

How to maintain your fade

Skin fades look best when they're fresh. Most clients book every 2–3 weeks to keep the blend crisp. Between visits, use a small amount of matte clay or pomade on top, and comb the sides daily to keep the shape.
